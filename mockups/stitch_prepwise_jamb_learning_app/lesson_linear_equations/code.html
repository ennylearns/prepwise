Introduction to Linear Equations

Understand the fundamental concepts of linear equations and how to solve for unknown variables.

A linear equation is an equation for a straight line. These are arguably the most important equations in algebra and serve as the foundation for more complex mathematical concepts. The main characteristic of a linear equation is that the highest power of the variable (usually x) is 1.

key Key Characteristics

  • circle The variables do not have exponents greater than 1.
  • circle The variables are not multiplied together.
  • circle When graphed on a Cartesian plane, it always forms a straight line.

The standard form of a linear equation in one variable is generally written as ax + b = c, where 'x' is the variable, and 'a', 'b', and 'c' are constants. The goal is to isolate 'x' on one side of the equation to find its value.

lightbulb

Worked Example

Solve the equation: 3x - 5 = 10

Step 1: Add 5 to both sides to isolate the term with x.

3x - 5 + 5 = 10 + 5

Step 2: Simplify.

3x = 15

Step 3: Divide by 3 to solve for x.

x = 5

Remember, whatever operation you perform on one side of the equation, you must perform the exact same operation on the other side to keep the equation balanced.
